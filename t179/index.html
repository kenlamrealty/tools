Four things to know

The city has to allow itState law says a certified manufactured home on a permanent foundation must be allowed on any lot zoned for a single-family home. The city can only control the roof overhang, roofing and siding, and no tougher than for a regular house (Gov. Code §65852.3).
It can be your ADUState ADU law counts a manufactured home as an ADU (Gov. Code §66313). Same size limits and fee rules as any ADU: no city impact fees at 750 sqft or less.
The 10-year catchA city may refuse a home that is more than 10 years old at the time you apply for the permit. Used homes: ask the planning counter first, before you buy.
Foundation turns it into real estateOnce the home is on a permanent foundation, the building department records HCD form 433A and HCD cancels the vehicle-style title. From then on the home is part of the land, taxed and sold like a house. That's T174.

Many people get this wrong: "A manufactured home is the cheap way to an ADU." The box is cheap. The site isn't. Foundation, sewer, power, crane and permits can run as much as the home itself on a tight San Jose lot. It still usually beats stick-built, just by less than the brochure says.
