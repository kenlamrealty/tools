Buying first? The base can't move until the old home is sold. Until then you get tax bills on the full price. After you sell and file, the assessor corrects it. Ask them how any refund works.
Disaster victims: use the old home's value right before the fire or disaster, not the damaged sale price. Disaster transfers don't count toward the 3-time limit.

    Next steps

    1. Find your factored base value on your tax bill or the assessor's site (Santa Clara: sccassessor.org).
    2. Plan the timing. Sell and buy within 2 years. Buying after you sell gives you more room (105% or 110%).
    3. After closing, get the claim form from the assessor of the county where the new home is. File the homeowners' exemption too (T131).
    4. Selling first? See what you walk away with in T21, and the tax on your gain in T22.

    Giving the home to your kids instead?

    That's a different Prop 19 rule. Since Feb 16, 2021, the parent-child exclusion covers only the family home, and only if the child moves in as their primary home. Value above the old assessed value plus about $1.04 million gets reassessed. This tool doesn't calculate it. Read the BOE Prop 19 page and talk to a CPA or estate attorney.

    Many people get this wrong: “Prop 19 only works in the same county.” Not anymore. Since April 1, 2021, you can move your base from any California county to any other.
