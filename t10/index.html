    Terms that strengthen an offer beyond price

    Shorter contingencies

    Contingency periods for inspection, appraisal and the loan are set in the contract. A shorter timeline tells the seller you will move decisively. Read the disclosures and any pre-inspection first so a short window is safe.

    Risk: less time to back out with your deposit safe.

    Larger deposit (EMD)

    Risk: once contingencies are removed, more money is on the line. See T08.

    Rent-back for the seller

    Let the seller stay a short time after closing, free or at their cost. Many sellers need time to move or to close on their next home, and that can matter more to them than a few thousand dollars.

    Risk: you wait to move in. Ask your lender how long is allowed.

    Appraisal gap coverage

    Risk: that money is cash on top of your down payment. See T11.

    Next steps

    1. Get actual sold comps: same type, within about half a mile, sold in the last 3–6 months. Text Ken the address or ask your agent.
    2. Get a full pre-approval (T34) and set your walk-away number before offer day (T02). Decide it with a clear head, not late on offer night.
    3. Read the disclosure package and any pre-inspection before you write. That tells you how short your contingencies can safely be (T70).
    4. Know your cash if the appraisal comes in low (T11). Plain-language buying guides: CFPB.

    Many people get this wrong: "Offering under list always saves money." Not on a home with several offers. An under-list offer often does not even get a counter, and you lose the home without saving anything. Single-family homes in Santa Clara County sold at about 103.6% of list in September 2026.
