HUD Fair Market Rent is not market rent. It is the 40th percentile of recent rents across the metro area, utilities included, and it sets voucher payment levels. Use it as a floor. In higher-priced areas such as Cupertino, Palo Alto, Fremont's Mission San Jose and Pleasanton, actual rents often run well above it. Alameda uses HUD's FY2027 figures, effective October 1, 2026. Santa Clara still uses FY2026 because HUD kept FY2026 in effect there while it reviews a reevaluation request.

Before you list

  • Rent control check (T55)
  • Deposit limit and screening fees (T52)Most landlords: deposit capped at 1 month of rent since July 1, 2024. Qualifying small landlords: up to 2 months, but only 1 month if the tenant is a service member.
  • Lease checklist (T54)Required disclosures, move-in photos, and which utilities each side pays.
  • Cash flow at this rent (T146)Mortgage, property tax, insurance, repairs and vacancy measured against the rent.

Next steps

  1. Pull 5 to 10 comparable rentals within about a mile that listed in the last 30 days. The ones that leased quickly show you the real market.
  2. Screen every applicant with the same written criteria. In California, Section 8 and other rental assistance count as lawful income, and you cannot refuse an applicant for using them. Rules: CA Civil Rights Dept. · CA Landlord-Tenant Guide.
  3. Confirm whether the unit falls under AB 1482 or a city rent ordinance before you sign. That decides how much you can raise the rent later and what you need to end a tenancy: T55 · CA Attorney General.
  4. Still weighing rent versus sell? Compare both in T51.

A common mistake: “I can charge what the last tenant paid plus 10%.” There are two different situations. For a tenant already in place, AB 1482 limits increases to 5% plus CPI, never more than 10%. From August 1, 2026 to July 31, 2027 that is 8.6% in Santa Clara County and 8.8% in Alameda County, and less in San Jose rent-stabilized apartments (5%) and Oakland (2.3%). For a new tenant, the old rent does not matter. You price to today's market, which can land above or below that 10%.
