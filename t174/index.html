Putting a mobile home on a permanent foundation (433A)

It turns the home into real property, which makes a regular mortgage possible. It also costs money, takes months and cannot be undone. See whether it makes sense in your case.

Worth it?

Pros
  • Home and land become one parcel of real property under one deed
  • Opens FHA, conventional and CalHFA loans (if the home qualifies)
  • More buyers can finance it when you sell
  • Homeowners' exemption and standard title insurance
Cons
  • Thousands of dollars up front
  • The home cannot be moved without the consent of all parties
  • In-lieu homes move to county property tax, one-way
  • Months of permits and paperwork

A foundation alone does not guarantee a loan. FHA and Fannie Mae also require a HUD-code home (built after 6/15/1976), at least 400 sq ft, and the correct foundation certification. Check the home in T166.

    Next steps

    1. Pull the title and lien information from HCD first. A lender on the home must agree before the home can be attached to land.
    2. Before paying for plans, ask your lender which foundation certification it needs (FHA requires an engineer's certification under HUD's Permanent Foundations Guide).
    3. Call the building department with your address and ask for its manufactured home foundation checklist and fee schedule.
    4. See how the tax changes in T163, and which loans the home qualifies for in T166.

    A common misconception: "Pour a slab under my home in the park and it becomes a real house." Not on its own. To become real property under H&S Code §18551, you must own the land or hold a lease of 35 years or more. On a park space rented month to month, the home remains personal property, foundation or not.
