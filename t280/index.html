    A permit in plain terms

    A permit is the city's approval to build, with the understanding that it will inspect the work. You submit the plans, pay a fee, and an inspector visits at key points: before the walls are closed, and at the end. The last sign-off is the final. Without a final, the work is not complete on record. Issuing the permit only lets the work begin; the final is what confirms it met code.

    The 5 stages of every permit

      How long a permit stays active
      California: work must begin within 12 months of issuance (CRC R105.5.1, H&S §18938.6). After that, if work is suspended for more than 180 days between inspections, the permit can expire. The building official may grant extensions in writing, up to 180 days each, for good cause. Oakland is stricter: a major inspection at least every 6 months, and the full scope completed within 2 years. Each city adds its own rules. Check the dates in T285.
