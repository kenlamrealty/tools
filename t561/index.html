3-Day Notice to Pay Rent or Quit (PRQ)

The notice a housing provider serves when rent is late: pay the past-due rent within 3 court days, or move out.

What does this form say?

Think of it as a final bill with a timer on it. It tells the tenant: you owe this much rent, here is who to pay and how, and you have 3 days to pay or leave. The timer only runs on court days. Saturdays, Sundays and court holidays don’t count. If the tenant pays the full amount in time, it’s over. If not, the owner can file an eviction case, called an unlawful detainer (UD).

Who uses it, and when

  • Given by: the housing provider (owner) or their agent or property manager.
  • Given to: every tenant on the lease, “and any other occupants” — including a subtenant actually living there.
  • When: only after the rent is actually late under the lease.
  • Deadline: 3 days, starting the day after service, skipping Saturdays, Sundays and court holidays (CCP 1161(2)).

Read the form, part by part

Our own plain-language summary of each paragraph. Not the form text.

Instruction page (remove before serving)

C.A.R. says: talk to a local eviction attorney first, don’t use it for rent more than a year old, and don’t e-sign it. Two red flags. One: a loan backed by the federal government (FHA, VA, Fannie Mae, Freddie Mac) or Section 8 — the CARES Act may require a 30-day notice instead. Two: a city rent or eviction ordinance, which can add steps. This page stays with you.

Top of the form: who and where

Write every adult tenant’s name. The form adds “and any other occupants” for you. Full address with the unit number. If the tenant gets mail somewhere else, there’s a line for that. A missing unit number is a cheap mistake that can cost you a month.

¶1 · 3 days to pay or leave

The core sentence. Within 3 days — counting from the day after service and skipping weekends and court holidays — the tenant must pay the past-due rent, or move out and give the place back. The tool below finds the exact last day.

¶2 · Rent due and how to pay

List each unpaid period with its amount, then the total. Rent only: no late fees, utilities, damages, or a fee for serving the notice (banned by SB 611). Then: who checks are made out to, the specific person who takes the rent, their phone and address, and the days and hours they take payment in person. The law also lets you give a bank account or an agreed electronic payment method instead. The form says: nothing more than 1 year old without a lawyer.

¶3 · If the tenant doesn’t pay

The owner can sue for possession, the unpaid rent, and a penalty of up to $600 if the court finds the tenant acted with malice (CCP 1174). The owner “declares a forfeiture”: if the rent stays unpaid and the tenant stays, the lease ends. It also warns that a negative credit report may be filed.

Signature lines

The owner or agent signs and dates it. Ink on paper — the form says no electronic signatures.

¶4 · Delivery and proof of service

Who served it, on what date, and how. The order is strict. A: hand it to the tenant. B: if they aren’t at home or work, leave it with a responsible adult there and mail a copy. C: only if A and B fail, post it somewhere visible on the property and mail a copy. Email doesn’t count. The person who served signs under penalty of perjury. Keep a copy; the judge will want it.

Why it is filled in this way

  • The $150 late fee is left out on purpose. It goes on a separate Notice to Cure (PCQ), never here.
  • October shows only what is still owed ($1,200), not the full month. Overstating by even a dollar can sink the notice.
  • The count: Mon 11/9 is day 1, Tue 11/10 is day 2, Wed 11/11 is Veterans Day (court closed), Thu 11/12 is day 3.

Next steps

  1. Put two dates on the calendar: the last day, and the first day you may file. Filing on the last day itself is a classic dismissal.
  2. Late fees, utilities or damage charges? Use a separate notice to cure: T562 · PCQ explained.
  3. Tenant moving out instead? Use the move-in / move-out checklist (T54), and see California rental law basics (T53). All forms: T560.

Many people get this wrong

“Add the late fee, it’s in the lease.” No. A PRQ is rent only. One wrong dollar and the judge can toss the whole case. You start over, a month later.

“3 days means Friday.” Weekends and court holidays don’t count. Serve on a Friday and day 1 is Monday.

“I texted them a photo of it.” Text and email are not service. Hand it over, or follow step B then C, and fill in the proof of service.
