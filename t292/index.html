    Many people get this wrong

    "The listing says approximate, so the number doesn't matter." It matters. In Furla v. Jon Douglas Co. (1998) the MLS said about 5,500 sqft; appraisers later measured roughly 4,400 to 4,600. The court said a gap over 20% is not minor, label or no label. The buyer still has to check: the law expects reasonable care from the buyer too (Civ. Code 2079.5).

    "My agent will catch any permit problem." The agent's walk-through looks at what can be seen. By law it does not cover public records or permits (Civ. Code 2079.3). That part is a separate job, with a deadline. This notice puts it on paper.

    "Signing this means I accept the house as is." No. Signing only shows you got the notice. Your contract, your contingency and the C.A.R. forms decide your rights.
