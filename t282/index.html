Why these points
MLS is smaller than the county figure. That is usually not a permit concern; the agent may have measured differently, or part of the house may have been removed.
This is a screening score, not a legal finding. The weights are an estimate. Only the city's permit records show what was approved.

What to do next

    Why county sqft is not a permit

    • The assessor taxes what it observes. The building department approves what is built. Two agencies, two separate records.
    • Santa Clara County states that its property characteristics are kept solely for assessment purposes and are not continuously updated. Alameda County states they are not part of the assessment roll.
    • A match does not prove a permit, and a gap does not prove there is none. Only the permit history on the city portal answers that.

    A common mistake: "The county shows 1,850 sqft, so all 1,850 are permitted." Not necessarily. The assessor can pick up an addition through a sale or a field review and tax it, even if no permit was ever pulled. Lenders and appraisers rely on the city record, not the tax bill.
