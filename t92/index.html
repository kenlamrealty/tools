"Other city" follows the state code (CRC R105.2). Your city can be stricter, so call its building department before you start.

Why it matters when you sell

  • The seller disclosure form (TDS) asks directly whether any additions, alterations or repairs were made without permits. You have to answer.
  • Selling within 18 months of buying? You must also disclose contractor work done during that time and give the buyer copies of any permits obtained (Civil Code 1102.6h).
  • Appraisers usually do not count unpermitted square footage, and insurers can push back on a claim tied to unpermitted work.

A common misconception: "A small bathroom remodel doesn't need a permit." New tile, a vanity and paint in the same layout: usually not. Moving the toilet, adding a fan circuit or replacing the tub valve inside the wall is plumbing and electrical work, and it needs a permit even in a 5 x 8 bathroom.

Next steps

  1. Look up the house's permit history on the city portal (linked under each job above). Buyers' agents check it too.
  2. Hiring out? Check the license at cslb.ca.gov and have the contractor pull the permit in its name. An unlicensed handyman may take only jobs under $1,000 that need no permit.
  3. Keep the signed-off permit card and final inspection record in your house file for the day you sell.
  4. Already have unpermitted work? Ask the city about permitting existing work before you list, not during escrow. Price it with the repair cost tool.
