Next steps

  1. Deliver the deposit within the time your contract states (RPA ¶3D). Before wiring, call escrow at a number you looked up yourself, and never trust an email that changes wiring details.
  2. Put the contingency deadline on your calendar. Complete inspection, appraisal and loan approval before it. See the full timeline in T60.
  3. Removing contingencies means signing C.A.R. Form CR. From that point your deposit is at risk. Do not remove the loan contingency without written loan approval.
  4. Need to cancel? Do it in writing (C.A.R. Form CC) before the deadline, then both sides sign escrow's cancellation instructions. Verify any agent's license at DRE.

A common misunderstanding: "Escrow can just give my deposit back." It cannot. Escrow is neutral and releases funds only when buyer and seller both sign, or when a court or arbitrator decides (RPA ¶14H). Refusing to sign without a good-faith reason can cost up to $1,000 more plus attorney fees (Civil Code §1057.3).
