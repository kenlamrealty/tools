Listing a rental building or commercial property? That's Form CLA.

Form CLA is the contract between the owner and the listing broker for commercial property or residential income property of any size. Here is every paragraph, then your numbers and dates.

What each part says

Tap a part to open it. Same order as the real form.

1Exclusive authorization

The owner hires the broker and gives an exclusive, irrevocable right to sell, lease, exchange, option, or something else (you check which) for the Listing Period. The form is for commercial property or residential income property of any number of units. It names the city, county, ZIP and the Assessor's Parcel Number.

Example: Owner A gives Brokerage Y the exclusive right to SELL the fourplex at 1234 Example Lane, San Jose, Santa Clara County, CA 95100, APN 000-00-000. "Exclusive" means: even if Owner A finds the buyer alone, the commission is still owed.

2AListing Period and price

Paragraph 2 is a table of the deal terms; the later paragraphs explain them. 2A(1): the listing starts on one date and ends at 11:59 P.M. on another. If the property has one to four units and is not owned by an entity, the period may not exceed 24 months; if it does, the agreement is void, unless the owner is a corporation, LLC or partnership. 2A(2): the listing price.

Example: 12/01/2026 to 05/31/2027 at 11:59 P.M., listed at $2,400,000. A 3-year listing on this fourplex owned by a person or a trust would be void (Civil Code §1670.12). Trusts are not on the exception list.

2BSpecial kinds of listings

Check a box if the property is a manufactured (mobile) home, then attach Form MHLA; or if it is sold in a probate, conservatorship or guardianship, then attach Form PLA.

Example: If 1234 Example Lane came from an estate in probate, the box is checked and PLA rides along (see T474).

2CCompensation

The form first says commissions are not fixed by law and can be negotiated; Form BCA is attached. C(1): pay to the owner's broker, as a % of the listing price (based on the purchase price once a purchase agreement is signed), plus any other amount, or a flat $, or the broker's own schedule. C(2): optional extra pay if the buyer or tenant has no agent, as a % of the acquisition price; it does not apply to dual agency. If the broker represents both sides, a separate written agreement with the buyer is needed for the buyer's side. C(3): the Continuation Period, a number of calendar days after the listing ends. C(4): earlier brokers the owner may still owe. C(5): a box if the broker pays a referral fee (Form RAD).

Example: 2.5% and the fourplex sells for $2,400,000: $60,000 to Brokerage Y. Buyer shows up with no agent and C(2) says 0.5%: $12,000 more. Continuation Period: 90 days.

2DWhat stays, what goes

Lists items the owner intends to include or exclude, items that are leased (propane tank, solar, water softener, alarm…), items with a lien because they were financed (HVAC, solar, windows or doors…), and smart-home features to include or exclude.

Example: Washers and dryers in the laundry room stay; the solar panels are leased, so the lease papers go to the buyer. Paragraph 5 explains the rules.

2EMLS and public marketing

E(1): which MLS the property goes on (primary and others); Form MLSA applies if an MLS is used or for residential 1–4. E(2): if checked, the broker may advertise that the owner will consider offers asking for concessions, in the MLS (if allowed) and/or elsewhere, with no amount stated unless the owner says so in writing. E(3): a box telling the broker not to use photos in marketing, except what MLS rules require.

Example: Primary MLS: MLSListings. E(2) checked for the MLS only. No amount shown.

2FOffers, buyer letters, reports

F(1): present offers as soon as practicable, or hold them until a date or a number of days after the listing goes active. F(2): by default the broker does not present buyer (transferee) letters; if the owner asks for them, the owner acts against the broker's advice. F(3): reports the owner will pay for, such as natural hazard disclosure, pest, general inspection, HOA documents, preliminary title report, roof, pool, septic/sewer, within 5 days (or another number) of the Beginning Date.

Example: Offers reviewed 10 days after going active. No buyer letters. Owner A orders NHD, pest and roof reports and pays by 12/06/2026.

2GTitle exceptions, opt-outs, extra terms

2G: exceptions to ownership or title (who else is on title, for example). A box if the owner plans to make the sale depend on buying a replacement property (Form SPRP). 2J: owner opt-outs, a lockbox and/or a sign. Then lines for additional terms.

Example: Owner A is doing a 1031 exchange and wants to buy the next building first: SPRP box checked. Tenants live there, so no sign.

3Advisories and addenda

Check which advisories come with the listing (Broker Compensation Advisory BCA, REO Advisory, Trust Advisory, other) and which addenda become part of it (Short Sale Listing Addendum and others).

Example: BCA and Trust Advisory checked, because the fourplex is held in a family trust.

4When the commission is earned

4A–B: commissions include all broker fees and are negotiable; the owner pays the 2C(1) amount. 4C: the extra amount in 2C(2) if no other brokerage is involved because the buyer has no agent. 4D, earned in three cases. (1) During the listing anyone, even the owner, brings a ready, willing and able buyer whose offer the owner accepts, and the buyer completes the deal or is stopped by the owner; it is owed even if escrow closes after the listing ends. (2) Within the Continuation Period (or the same time after a cancellation), the owner contracts with someone, or their related entity, who was physically shown the property during the listing or whose signed offer the broker delivered, but only if the broker gave the owner a written list of names (Form NPB) before the listing expired. (3) The owner, without the broker's written consent, withdraws, transfers, leases or makes the property unmarketable during the listing.

Example: Investor C toured 1234 Example Lane in March. The listing ends 05/31/2027 and Brokerage Y gives Owner A an NPB with Investor C's name on 05/28. Owner A sells to Investor C on 07/10. That is inside 90 days, so the commission is owed.

4E–FBuyer backs out; earlier brokers

4E(1): if someone other than the owner blocks the sale, the commission is paid only if and when the owner collects damages, and then the lesser of half the damages or the commission, after title, escrow and collection costs. 4E(2): the owner irrevocably assigns the commission from escrow proceeds. 4F: the owner says there is no earlier listing and no duty to pay another broker except as listed in 2C(4); if the property sells to one of those people while that duty runs, this broker earns nothing and need not represent the owner in that deal.

Example: A buyer walks away and Owner A keeps a $40,000 deposit as damages, with $2,000 in escrow costs. Half of $38,000 is $19,000, less than $60,000, so the broker gets $19,000.

5Items included, leased, liened, smart home

Unless agreed otherwise, attached fixtures go with the property and personal property does not. What 2D says is only the owner's intent: the purchase agreement decides, and the broker doesn't guarantee it. For leased or liened items, the owner gives the buyer copies of the lease or loan papers.

Example: A ceiling fan is bolted on, so it stays. A sofa is not. The solar lease gets handed over.

6What the owner promises

The owner says that, unless stated in writing, they know of no notice of default, no late loan payments, no bankruptcy, no lawsuit or government action affecting the property or the sale, and no special assessments. If any of these come up during the listing, the owner tells the broker in writing right away.

Example: The city sends a notice of a sidewalk assessment in January. Owner A emails Brokerage Y the same week.

7Broker's and owner's duties

7A: the broker uses reasonable effort and may (not must) order reports, advertise in any media including MLS and internet, and tell other agents that offers came in and at what price. 7B: strategies for presenting offers, including that some buyers dislike a fixed review date and may send a short-fuse pre-emptive offer, and some can't pay their own agent and may ask the owner to. The broker presents offers right away unless told in 2F(1) to hold them; then the broker says an offer arrived but holds it. 7C: buyer letters may reveal protected traits; deciding on that basis is unlawful; the broker won't read them. 7D: the owner considers offers in good faith, allows showings, refers inquiries, and decides the price. 7E: the owner orders and pays for the 2F(3) reports; for HOA property, getting HOA documents early can cut cost and delay. 7F: the owner protects the broker from claims caused by wrong information or known defects the owner hid.

Example: Owner A knew unit 3 had a slow roof leak and didn't say. The buyer sues. Under 7F, Owner A covers Brokerage Y's costs.

8Deposit

The broker may accept and hold, for the owner, deposits that go toward the price.

Example: The buyer's deposit check goes to escrow through Brokerage Y.

9Agency: who the broker works for

9A: the owner received the agency disclosure (AD). 9B: the broker represents the owner. 9C: dual agency may happen; the owner consents if the buyer comes through the broker or an agent in the same firm. A dual agent won't tell the buyer the owner would take less than the list price, or tell the owner the buyer would pay more, without written consent. Brokers must have a representation agreement with a buyer before showing residential property; the owner consents, and the firm then becomes a dual agent. The broker may list competing properties (PRBS). 9D: unrepresented buyers sign a refusal and get a non-agency disclosure (BNA or OHNA-SI). 9E: agency is confirmed in writing before the sale agreement. 9F: agency ends when the listing expires or the deal completes, whichever is first.

Example: Agent Z, also at Brokerage Y, brings Investor C. Brokerage Y is now a dual agent. Agent Z can't tell Investor C that Owner A would take $2,300,000.

10Owner concessions

Concessions are money the owner puts toward the buyer's costs: escrow, title, lender fees, repairs, inspections, even the buyer's broker. Concessions shown in the MLS must be usable for any allowed buyer cost and can't say "for broker pay"; but a buyer's offer may ask the owner to pay the buyer's broker. The listing broker won't confirm that amount is really owed. MLS concessions are not promises: they bind only if written into the accepted contract.

Example: MLS says "seller will consider concessions." Investor C asks for $15,000. Owner A counters $8,000, and that is the only number that counts.

11Security, insurance, cameras

The broker is not responsible for loss, damage or injury from the lockbox, showings or otherwise. Appraisers, inspectors, agents and buyers may take photos and video inside. The owner protects valuables and gets insurance; the broker has none for the owner. Visitors may not know about owner cameras, so the owner should post a notice.

Example: Owner A has a doorbell camera that records sound: a small sign at the door, done.

12Photos and internet ads

Unless 2E(3) is checked, the broker or others may photograph and capture the property for tours, the broker's site, MLS and marketing. Third-party photographers may keep some rights. Once online, nobody controls who sees or reuses the images. The owner assigns image rights to the broker/agent, who may use them later, even after the sale. Visitors' own photos can't be blocked. Digitally altered images must say so and link to the original, or show the original next to it on the broker's own site (B&P Code §10140.8).

Example: The empty unit is virtually staged. The flyer says "virtually staged" with a QR code to the real photo.

13–14Lockbox and sign

A lockbox lets brokers, MLS members, inspectors and accompanied buyers in. The broker may let in inspectors, appraisers and workers without asking again. Nobody insures against theft or damage from the lockbox. The owner allows a lockbox and a FOR SALE/SOLD/LEASE sign unless opted out in 2J. If the owner doesn't live there, the owner gets the tenants' written permission for the lockbox (Form KLA).

Example: All four units are rented. Owner A gets a signed KLA from each tenant before the lockbox goes on.

15–16Equal housing; attorney fees

The property is offered under federal, state and local anti-discrimination laws. In a dispute between owner and broker, each pays their own attorney, except as paragraph 19A says.

Example: No "ideal for young professionals" in the ad. Describe the building, not the buyer.

17Management approval

If an agent (salesperson or broker associate) signs this listing for the broker, the broker or manager may cancel it in writing within 5 days after it is signed.

Example: Signed 12/01/2026; Brokerage Y's manager can still cancel through 12/06/2026.

18–20Successors; entire agreement

18: the listing binds the owner's successors and assigns. 20: this paper replaces all earlier talks and deals; spoken side promises don't count. If one part is invalid, the rest still stands. Copies and counterparts are fine.

Example: The agent "promised" a 1% fee over coffee, but the form says 2.5%. The form wins.

19Disputes: mediation first

19A: owner and broker mediate before arbitration or court; fees split equally. Whoever sues without trying mediation, or refuses it, can't recover attorney fees even if they win. 19B: not covered: foreclosure, unlawful detainer (eviction), mechanic's liens, probate, small claims or bankruptcy court matters; filing for provisional remedies doesn't break the rule. 19C: arbitration only if both sign Form ARB.

Example: Owner A and Brokerage Y disagree on the commission. A mediator, $1,000 fee, $500 each.

21–22Ownership and authorized signer

21: the owner warrants they own it, no one else is on title, and they can sign and sell; exceptions go in 2G. 22: someone signing for a trust, company, estate or under a power of attorney signs in that role, not personally, says the entity exists and is in good standing, and gives the broker proof of authority within 3 days (trust certification under Probate Code §18100.5, letters testamentary, court order, power of attorney, corporate resolution…).

Example: Owner A signs as trustee of the "Example Family Trust" and sends the trust certification by 12/04/2026.

✎Signatures and the boxes at the end

An entity-owner box with the full entity name, how it will be called, and the authorized signers (then Form RCSD isn't needed). Owner signatures with address, email, phone; Form ASA for more owners. Broker firm, DRE number, and up to two agents. Two last boxes: AAA if several agents of one firm represent the owner, ABA if two brokerages co-list.

Example: Agent X and Agent Z of Brokerage Y both sign, and the AAA box is checked (see T460).

+Papers that come in the same packet

The packet this page was read from also has: AD (agency disclosure, Revised 12/24), MLSA (MLS addendum, Revised 12/25: Clear Cooperation means a residential 1–4 or lot listing goes into the MLS within 1 business day of public marketing; marketing options like full exposure, delayed marketing, coming soon, office exclusive), PRBS-S (consent to the broker representing more than one seller and to dual agency, 6/26), BCA (broker compensation advisory, 6/25), FHDA (fair housing, 12/24) and CCPA (privacy; brokers and MLSs keep records 3 years, 6/26).

Example: Owner A wants a quiet sale to agents' clients only: that's the office-exclusive option in MLSA, and any public ad starts the 1-business-day clock.

What many people get wrong

"Once the listing expires, I owe nothing." Not quite. Paragraph 4D(2): sell within the Continuation Period to someone who toured or made an offer during the listing, and was named on the NPB list before it expired, and the commission is owed.

"I'll just pull it off the market. No harm." Under 4D(3), withdrawing, leasing or transferring the property during the listing without the broker's written consent earns the commission. Ask for a written cancellation first.

"Read the buyers' letters, they help you pick." My blunt view after 23 years: don't read them. The form says letters can reveal protected traits and choosing on that is unlawful (7C). A clean offer comparison beats a sweet letter every time.
