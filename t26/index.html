Next steps

  1. Get two prices from a local agent: as-is and fixed up. That gap tells you if fixing is worth it. Plug them into the seller net sheet.
  2. If you go cash: ask for a written offer, proof of funds, who pays closing costs, and whether they can assign the contract to someone else.
  3. If you fix: pick only the repairs that pay back with the fix-before-selling tool, follow the pre-sale checklist, and check contractors at cslb.ca.gov.
  4. Any way you sell, fill out the disclosures (TDS, SPQ, NHD). See the seller disclosure guide.

Many people get this wrong: "Selling as-is means I don't have to disclose." False in California. The law says the Transfer Disclosure Statement cannot be waived in an as-is sale (Civil Code §1102.1). As-is only means you won't fix things; you still tell buyers what you know.
