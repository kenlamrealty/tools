California mover rules

  • The mover must hold a household goods permit from BHGS. Look it up at bhgs.dca.ca.gov.
  • A written estimate may be given only after the mover sees your belongings. In California, phone or internet estimates without a visual inspection are illegal.
  • Every household move needs a "Not to Exceed" price, the most you can be charged, unless you request more services and sign a change order before the work.
  • Booked 3+ days ahead? You must receive the agreement and the "Important Notice About Your Move" at least 3 days before moving day.
  • Under 100 miles, movers may bill by the hour. Over 100 miles, pricing is by weight and distance.

A common misunderstanding: "The cheapest phone quote means the cheapest move." A low quote without seeing your home is how hostage-load schemes begin: the truck gets loaded, then the price jumps. In California the mover can collect only the Not to Exceed price plus change orders you signed. Without an inspection (preferably in person) and a written Not to Exceed price, do not sign.

Next steps

  1. Get 3 written Not to Exceed estimates after an inspection. Compare crew size and hours, not just the total.
  2. Check each mover's BHGS permit and complaint history before paying a deposit.
  3. Choose your protection level: free basic liability pays only 60¢ per pound per item. Ask for the price of full value protection.
  4. Plan the weeks ahead with the moving checklist, change your address with T102, and set up utilities with T130.
