Your rights as an applicant

  • Application screening fee: no more than $68.96 per applicant in 2026. The cap is adjusted for inflation each year.
  • The landlord must give you a receipt itemizing what the fee covered, and a copy of your credit report if you ask.
  • If no unit is available, the landlord cannot charge a screening fee unless you agree in writing. Any part of the fee not used for screening must be returned.
  • Deposit: since July 1, 2024, most landlords may collect no more than 1 month of rent. A small landlord (an individual, or an LLC owned by individuals, with no more than 2 rental properties and 4 units total) may collect up to 2 months, but only 1 month from a service member.

Next steps

  1. Pull your free credit reports at annualcreditreport.com before you apply, and dispute any errors first.
  2. Have your file ready: 2 recent pay stubs, last year's W-2 or tax return, photo ID, and 2 months of bank statements. A complete application gets reviewed first.
  3. Read the state's Landlord-Tenant Guide and the rent increase rules in T55.
  4. Paying this much in rent? Compare it with owning in T04: rent vs. buy.

A common mistake: “A landlord can ask for 2 or 3 months of deposit.” Not since July 1, 2024 (AB 12). Most landlords may collect a deposit of no more than 1 month, plus the first month's rent. Qualifying small landlords may collect up to 2 months. Three months is no longer allowed.
