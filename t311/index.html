Business days here: Monday–Friday, excluding federal holidays (assuming the lender's office is closed Saturday). The 7-day waiting period counts Saturdays. If the LE is mailed, it is treated as received 3 business days later.

The six items that make an “application”

  1. Your name
  2. Your income
  3. Your Social Security number (to pull credit)
  4. The property address
  5. An estimate of the home's value
  6. The loan amount you want

Once the lender has these six items, the 3-day clock starts. A lender cannot require a purchase contract or pay stubs first just to issue a Loan Estimate.

A Loan Estimate is not an approval. At this stage the lender has neither approved nor denied you. It only shows the terms you would likely receive if you proceed.
Before you state your intent to proceed: the only fee a lender may charge is a reasonable credit report fee. No application fee and no appraisal fee yet. Silence does not count as intent; you have to tell them.

This is our own rendering of the three-page Loan Estimate, not the official CFPB form. Section names follow the rule (12 CFR 1026.37). Numbers are estimates for a Santa Clara County purchase. Your lender's LE is the document that counts.

Red flags

  • The lender wants an application or appraisal fee before you receive the LE and state your intent to proceed. Only a credit report fee is allowed.
  • No LE within 3 business days after you provided the six items, or you are told to “send your contract first”.
  • The rate lock says “NO” but the rate is presented as guaranteed. Get the lock in writing.
  • Section A on one LE is far above the others at the same rate. That fee cannot come down later; negotiate it now.
  • The APR is much higher than the rate. Ask which fees are included.
  • A prepayment penalty or balloon payment shows YES on a standard home loan.
  • Anyone emails you new wiring instructions. Call escrow at a number you already know.
