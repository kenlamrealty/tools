Five ways it differs from a house

You own the home, not the landThe home is personal property titled through HCD, not the DMV, and it has no grant deed. The land belongs to the park.
Space rent goes up
The park must approve the buyerManagement may check that the buyer can pay the rent and charges. It must answer in writing within 15 business days after receiving a complete application (Civil Code §798.74).
Selling needs park approval tooYour buyer goes through the same approval. With fewer lenders and fewer qualified buyers, a mobile home can take longer to sell.
Value often does not grow like a houseIn a park, most appreciation goes to the land, which you do not own. Many homes hold flat or lose value with age, and rising space rent tends to pull resale prices down.

Next steps

  1. Before any offer, ask the park for the rental agreement, the park rules, the last three years of rent increases and its buyer approval criteria.
  2. Check the home's title and tax status with HCD Registration & Titling. Unpaid fees and taxes stay with the home.
  3. Park in San Jose? Call the city's Rent Stabilization Program at 408-975-4480 to confirm the space is covered by the Mobilehome Rent Ordinance.
  4. Get chattel quotes from two or three lenders, then compare with a condo in T01.

A common misconception: "mobile homes are cheap." The home may be. The land is not. San Jose space rent averaged about $1,100 a month in 2022, and market-rate spaces in Castro Valley were quoted at $1,300 to $1,450 in 2023. Over 20 years, the rent can add up to more than the home itself.
